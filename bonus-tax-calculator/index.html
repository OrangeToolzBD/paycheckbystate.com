Bonus Tax Calculator

A bonus tax calculator shows what a bonus check keeps after withholding. Federal withholding is usually a flat 22% (37% on bonuses above $1 million) under IRS Publication 15 (2026), plus 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare. A $5,000 bonus with no state withholding leaves $3,517.50.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Paycheck by State

How are bonuses taxed?

The IRS treats a bonus as supplemental wages. Your employer withholds federal income tax, usually at a flat 22%, plus Social Security at 6.2% and Medicare at 1.45% (IRS Topic 751, updated January 20, 2026). State income tax comes on top in states that tax wages.

Here is the $5,000 example, assuming $60,000 already paid this year and no state withholding:

ItemRateAmount
Bonus$5,000.00
Federal income tax withholding22%−$1,100.00
Social Security6.2%−$310.00
Medicare1.45%−$72.50
Bonus after withholding$3,517.50

Two caps change the math. Social Security stops once your 2026 wages pass $184,500 (the same IRS page), so a $10,000 bonus paid after $190,000 of wages owes none, just $145 of Medicare. Medicare adds 0.9% on wages above $200,000. States set their own bonus withholding rules, so the tool asks for your state’s rate rather than guessing it.

Why is my bonus withheld at 22%?

Your bonus is withheld at 22% because IRS Publication 15 (2026) lets employers use that flat rate on supplemental wages paid separately from regular pay. It is a withholding shortcut, not your tax rate. Once supplemental wages pass $1 million in a year, 37% is mandatory on the excess.

An employer can use the flat rate only if it withheld income tax from your regular wages in the current or previous year. The alternative is to add the bonus to a regular paycheck and withhold on the total as if it were a single payment. That can take a larger share, because the combined check looks like a higher annual salary to the withholding tables.

The 22% figure lines up with the 2026 bracket that single filers reach at $50,400 of taxable income (IRS, October 9, 2025). If your income sits below that bracket, 22% overshoots your real rate on the bonus; above it, it can fall short.

With $1,200,000 of bonuses and none earlier in the year, the first $1 million is withheld at 22% ($220,000) and the other $200,000 at 37% ($74,000).

Is a bonus taxed more than salary at tax time?

No. When you file, your bonus is simply added to your wages and taxed at the same bracket rates as salary. The flat 22% was only a prepayment. If it was more than the tax the bonus actually adds, the extra counts toward a refund; if less, you owe the difference.

Take a single filer earning $65,000 with the 2026 standard deduction of $16,100 and no other income. Federal income tax is $5,620. Add a $5,000 bonus and it becomes $6,570, so the bonus adds $950, about 19%, because part of it falls in the 12% bracket. Withholding took $1,100, so $150 more than needed was prepaid.

At higher incomes the gap reverses. For a single filer already in the 24% bracket, which starts at $105,700 of taxable income, each bonus dollar adds 24 cents of tax against 22 cents withheld. Frequently asked questions

Are bonuses usually taxed 40%?

Not by federal withholding alone. The flat 22% plus 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare adds up to 29.65% of the bonus while your wages are under the Social Security cap. State withholding, where your state taxes wages, comes on top. The tax you finally owe on the bonus depends on your bracket when you file, which can be higher or lower than what was withheld.

How much is a $10,000 bonus after taxes?

About $7,035 before state tax, if your wages are under the Social Security cap. Federal withholding at 22% is $2,200, Social Security $620 and Medicare $145. If you enter a 5% state rate in the calculator, $500 more comes out and you keep $6,535. Your final tax is settled on your return.

How do you calculate taxes on a bonus?

Multiply the bonus by 22% for federal withholding (37% on any part above $1 million for the year), by 6.2% for Social Security (only up to the $184,500 wage base) and by 1.45% for Medicare. Add your state’s bonus rate if it has one. Subtract the total from the bonus to get what lands in your account.

Why did my bonus get taxed at 35%?

Usually because state withholding sits on top of the 29.65% federal and payroll share. With a 5% state rate entered, a $10,000 bonus loses $3,465, or 34.65%. Another cause is the aggregate method, where your employer adds the bonus to a regular paycheck, which can withhold more. Wages above $200,000 also add 0.9% Medicare.
