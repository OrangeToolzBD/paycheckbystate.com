Overtime Calculator

An overtime calculator multiplies your overtime hours by 1.5 times your regular rate (or 2 times for double time) and adds regular pay. Federal law requires time and a half for covered workers after 40 hours in a workweek (U.S. Department of Labor). At $20 an hour, 50 hours pays $1,100 before tax.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Paycheck by State

How is overtime pay calculated?

Overtime pay is overtime hours × regular hourly rate × 1.5. Add that to regular hours × regular rate for the week’s gross pay. At $20 an hour, the overtime rate is $30, so 40 regular hours plus 10 overtime hours pays $800 + $300 = $1,100 before taxes.

Two federal rules shape the count. A workweek is “a fixed and regularly recurring period of 168 hours,” which can start on any day, and hours cannot be averaged across two or more weeks (DOL overtime pay page). So 50 hours one week and 30 the next still earns 10 overtime hours.

Who has to be paid overtime?

Employees covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act who are not exempt must get overtime for hours over 40 in a workweek. Many salaried managers and professionals are exempt, but only if their duties qualify and they are paid a salary of at least $684 a week, the level in the Labor Department’s fact sheet.

According to DOL Fact Sheet 17A (revised September 2019), executive, administrative, professional and outside sales employees generally must meet tests about their job duties and be paid on a salary basis of not less than $684 a week to be exempt. Job title alone does not decide it.

Other points from the DOL overtime page:

  • The FLSA does not require extra pay for Saturdays, Sundays, holidays or regular days of rest unless the hours push the week past 40.
  • The federal standard is weekly. It sets no daily overtime threshold, although some states do.

Is overtime taxed more?

No. Overtime is taxed at the same rates as the rest of your wages. A paycheck with overtime can show a higher share withheld because the withholding tables treat that larger check as if you earned it every period, but the final tax is settled on your return.

For 2025 through 2028 there is also a federal deduction for qualified overtime. The IRS fact sheet FS-2025-03 (updated July 25, 2025) sets these terms:

  • It covers only pay above your regular rate, such as the “half” portion of time and a half, and only overtime the FLSA requires.
  • The yearly maximum is $12,500, or $25,000 for joint filers.
  • It phases out above $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income ($300,000 joint).
  • You need a Social Security number on the return and must file jointly if married. It is available whether or not you itemize.

It is a deduction you claim when you file, not a change to the tax withheld from each paycheck. In the $20-an-hour example, the qualified part of 10 overtime hours is 10 × $10 = $100. Daily overtime rules in some states

Some states require overtime by the day as well as by the week. California is the best-known example: time and a half after 8 hours in a workday and double time after 12, with extra rules for the seventh consecutive day. Where state and federal rules differ, check your state labor agency.

The California Department of Industrial Relations overtime FAQ sets these rates:

  • 1.5× for hours over 8 and up to 12 in a workday, and for the first 8 hours on the seventh consecutive day worked in a workweek.
  • 2× for hours over 12 in a workday, and for hours over 8 on that seventh consecutive day.
  • Hours over 40 in the workweek also earn overtime.

Worked examples at $20 an hour in California:

  • One 10-hour day: 8 × $20 + 2 × $30 = $220.
  • One 13-hour day: 8 × $20 + 4 × $30 + 1 × $40 = $320.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my overtime pay?

Multiply your regular hourly rate by 1.5, then by the hours you worked past 40 in the workweek. Add regular pay for the first 40 hours. At $18 an hour with 45 hours worked, overtime is 5 × $27 = $135, regular pay is $720, and the week totals $855 before taxes.

How do I work out my OT rate?

Multiply your regular hourly rate by 1.5 for time and a half, or by 2 for double time. A $20 regular rate gives a $30 overtime rate and a $40 double-time rate; a $25 rate gives $37.50 and $50. Use the regular rate, not an average that already includes overtime.

Is there a new overtime tax rule?

Yes. For tax years 2025 through 2028, workers can deduct qualified overtime pay, meaning the premium above their regular rate that federal law requires, up to $12,500 a year ($25,000 joint). It phases out above $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income ($300,000 joint) and is claimed on the tax return, according to IRS fact sheet FS-2025-03.
