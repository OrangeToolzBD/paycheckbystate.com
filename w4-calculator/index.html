W-4 Withholding Calculator

A W-4 calculator checks whether your paychecks will cover your 2026 federal income tax. On a $90,000 single salary paid every two weeks, this tool estimates $10,970 of tax and $421.92 withheld per check, using the IRS Publication 15-T (2026) percentage method and the 2026 IRS brackets.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Paycheck by State

How do I know if I am withholding enough?

You are withholding enough when the federal tax already taken this year, plus what your remaining paychecks will take, at least matches your estimated tax for the year after credits. If it falls short, the 2026 Form W-4 warns that you will generally owe tax when you file and may owe a penalty.

The calculator estimates your tax after the standard deduction and your credits, then adds the year-to-date withholding from your pay stub to the per-check amount at default W-4 settings times the paychecks left.

With one job and nothing else to report, the two usually land close, because Step 1(c) sets the standard deduction and rates payroll uses. Other income breaks the match. Add $10,000 of interest to the $90,000 example and the tax rises to $13,170 while withholding stays at $10,970: a $2,200 gap, or $84.62 extra on each of 26 checks.

What does each step of Form W-4 change?

Each step feeds your employer’s withholding worksheet. Filing status picks the tax table, Step 2 raises withholding for multiple jobs, Step 3 lowers it by your expected credits, and Step 4 adds other income, subtracts extra deductions or adds a flat amount per check.

How the Publication 15-T (2026) payroll worksheet uses each entry:

W-4 entryWhat payroll does with itEffect on each check
Step 1(c) filing statusPicks the table for your statusSets the base
Step 2 box not checkedSubtracts $8,600 from annual wages ($12,900 if married filing jointly)Lower withholding
Step 2 box checkedSkips that subtraction and uses the Step 2 tableHigher withholding
Step 3 creditsSubtracts the yearly amount ÷ pay periodsLower withholding
Step 4(a) other incomeAdds it to annual wagesHigher withholding
Step 4(b) deductionsSubtracts it from annual wagesLower withholding
Step 4(c) extra withholdingAdds it to each check as enteredHigher withholding

For example, $4,400 in Step 3 cuts biweekly withholding by about $169.23 ($4,400 ÷ 26). The paycheck calculator accepts every W-4 step.

What if I have two jobs?

With two or more jobs, or a working spouse when you file jointly, each employer withholds as if its paycheck were your only income, so the total often comes up short. The 2026 Form W-4 offers three fixes in Step 2, and you pick only one of them.

  • (a) the IRS estimator, the most accurate option;
  • (b) the Multiple Jobs Worksheet, whose result goes in Step 4(c) of the highest-paying job;
  • (c) for exactly two jobs, the Step 2 box checked on both forms, which suits jobs with similar pay.

From the form’s single-filer worksheet table, a $70,000 job plus a $45,000 job gives $7,100 a year, or $273.08 per biweekly check. The box alone matters too: on $90,000 single pay every two weeks, withholding is $421.92 unchecked and $614.12 checked.

This tool models one job, so for two jobs use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator.

When should I file a new W-4?

File a new W-4 whenever a change in your life or finances would change what you entered on the form, such as marriage, a new job for you or your spouse, a new dependent, or new deductions or credits. That is the rule printed in the 2026 Form W-4 instructions.

The form also suggests rechecking with the IRS estimator at the start of each year.

Under IRS Publication 15 (2026), your employer must put a new W-4 into effect no later than the start of the first payroll period ending on or after the 30th day after you hand it in. If you claimed exemption for 2026, the form says you need a new W-4 by February 16, 2027.

A pre-2020 W-4 does not need replacing, per the IRS W-4 FAQ. For every line explained, read how to fill out a W-4.

Frequently asked questions

How do I figure out what to put on my W-4?

Start with Step 1 and Step 5, which everyone completes; the form says to fill in Steps 2 to 4 only if they apply to you. For the dollar amounts, the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator takes about 25 minutes, asks for recent pay stubs, and produces a pre-filled W-4. This calculator gives a quicker one-job check.

How are W-4 withholdings calculated?

Your employer annualizes your pay, adds Step 4(a), and subtracts Step 4(b) plus $8,600 ($12,900 if married filing jointly) when the Step 2 box is unchecked. It applies the Publication 15-T table for your filing status, divides by your pay periods, subtracts your share of Step 3 credits and adds Step 4(c).

Do you still claim 0 or 1 on a W-4?

No. Allowances such as 0 or 1 are gone from the redesigned form used since 2020. The IRS explains that allowances were tied to the personal exemption, which current law eliminated. Today you enter dollar amounts for credits, other income, deductions and extra withholding instead of a number of allowances.

How do I work out a W-4 for a new job?

Fill out Steps 1 and 5, then add Steps 2 to 4 only if they apply. If you start after January, the form points you to the IRS estimator, since part-year pay changes the math. Here, enter the tax already withheld from earlier pay stubs and the paychecks left. Without a W-4, Publication 15 says you are treated as single with no adjustments.
