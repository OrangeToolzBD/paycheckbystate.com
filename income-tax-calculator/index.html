Income Tax Calculator 2026

This income tax calculator applies the 2026 federal brackets, 10% up to 37%, after the new standard deduction of $16,100 for single filers or $32,200 for joint filers (IRS, October 9, 2025). A single filer earning $80,000 owes an estimated $8,770 in federal tax, plus a state estimate.

What are the 2026 federal tax brackets?

The 2026 federal brackets are 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37%. For single filers the 12% rate starts above $12,400 of taxable income and the 37% rate above $640,600; for married couples filing jointly, above $24,800 and $768,700. The IRS published these thresholds on October 9, 2025.

How do tax brackets actually work?

Brackets are marginal: each rate applies only to the slice of taxable income inside its band. Moving into a higher bracket never raises the tax on dollars already earned below it, so a raise that crosses a threshold always leaves you with more after tax, not less.

Here is the calculator’s default case, a single filer with $80,000 of income and no other deductions in 2026:

  1. Subtract the $16,100 standard deduction: taxable income is $63,900.
  2. The first $12,400 is taxed at 10%: $1,240.
  3. The next $38,000 (from $12,400 to $50,400) is taxed at 12%: $4,560.
  4. The remaining $13,500 (from $50,400 to $63,900) is taxed at 22%: $2,970.
  5. Total federal income tax: $8,770.

Only $13,500 of this person’s income meets the 22% rate. The running totals below show the tax on all income up to the start of each single-filer bracket, which is a quick way to check any result by hand:

Marginal vs effective tax rate

Your marginal rate is the rate on your last dollar of taxable income; your effective rate is total tax divided by total income. For a single filer with $80,000 in 2026, the marginal rate is 22% but the effective federal rate is about 11.0%, because most income is taxed at 10% or 12% or not at all.

The two rates answer different questions:

  • Marginal rate tells you what a change is worth. With a 22% marginal rate, $5,000 of traditional 401(k) contributions cuts the $80,000 filer’s federal tax from $8,770 to $7,670, a $1,100 saving. Enter the contribution in the tool’s pre-tax field to see it.
  • Effective rate tells you your overall share. $8,770 ÷ $80,000 = 10.96%.

Filing status changes both. A married couple filing jointly on the same $80,000 has $47,800 of taxable income after the $32,200 deduction, owes $5,240, and sits in the 12% bracket with an effective rate of 6.55%.

State tax adds its own layer. On $80,000 of single-filer wages the tool’s 2026 estimates are about $3,210 in California, $3,723 in New York and $3,815 in Illinois, and nothing in Texas, using Tax Foundation’s 2026 brackets (February 17, 2026). These leave out local taxes and state credits. To see how the totals show up on each paycheck, use the paycheck calculator.

How much is the 2026 standard deduction?

The 2026 standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers and married people filing separately, $32,200 for married couples filing jointly, and $24,150 for heads of household, according to the IRS 2026 inflation adjustments released October 9, 2025. It is subtracted before the brackets apply.

Taxpayers 65 and older can also claim a separate deduction of $6,000 per eligible person for 2025 through 2028. The IRS says it is “in addition to the current additional standard deduction for seniors” and phases out above $75,000 of modified adjusted gross income ($150,000 for joint filers) (IRS fact sheet FS-2025-03, updated July 25, 2025).

The calculator uses the basic standard deduction only. It does not add the senior deductions or itemized deductions, so if either applies to you, your actual tax will be lower than the estimate shown. The method is described on the methodology page.

Frequently asked questions

What are the tax brackets for 2026?

The seven federal rates for 2026 are 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37%. For a single filer they start at taxable income over $0, $12,400, $50,400, $105,700, $201,775, $256,225 and $640,600. Joint filers’ thresholds are $24,800, $100,800, $211,400, $403,550, $512,450 and $768,700, per the IRS release of October 9, 2025.

What is the new $6,000 tax deduction for seniors?

It is an extra deduction of up to $6,000 for each taxpayer who is 65 or older, available for tax years 2025 through 2028. A married couple where both qualify can claim $12,000. It phases out above $75,000 of modified adjusted gross income, or $150,000 for joint filers, and the qualifying person’s Social Security number must be on the return, per IRS fact sheet FS-2025-03.

How do I calculate my federal income tax?

Start with your total taxable income, subtract the standard deduction ($16,100 single or $32,200 joint for 2026) or your itemized deductions, then apply each bracket rate only to the income within that bracket and add the pieces. Finally subtract any credits. A single filer with $80,000 of income and no credits owes $8,770.
