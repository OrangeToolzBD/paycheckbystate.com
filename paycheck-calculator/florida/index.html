Florida Paycheck Calculator

In Florida, a single worker earning $65,000 a year and paid every two weeks takes home about $2,092.60 per paycheck in 2026: $216.15 federal withholding (IRS Pub 15-T), $191.25 Social Security and Medicare, and no state income tax, because Florida does not tax wages.

Does Florida have local income taxes?

No. Florida has no state income tax on wages, and no Florida county or city adds one. The Tax Foundation’s 2026 state income tax table (February 17, 2026) lists Florida among the eight states that levy no individual income tax at all, and it does not name Florida among the states with county- or city-level income taxes.

That is why the Florida tax column in the take-home table above is $0.00 at $40,000, $65,000 and $100,000. The only income tax withheld from a Florida paycheck is federal, set by your Form W-4. Florida has no state withholding certificate to complete when you start a job.

One practical point for people who move: if you relocate to Florida from a state that taxes wages, your employer may still be withholding for the old state. Once you no longer live or work there, ask payroll to update your work and home addresses so that state withholding stops. If you work remotely for an employer based elsewhere, the rules of that other state can matter, so check with its revenue department.

To see how much a state income tax would change your check, compare Florida with any other state on the state income tax rates page.

What else comes out of a paycheck in Florida?

Florida paychecks carry the federal deductions only: income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare. The IRS sets the employee rates at 6.2% for Social Security and 1.45% for Medicare (page updated January 20, 2026), plus 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on wages above $200,000 in a year. IRS Publication 15 puts the 2026 Social Security wage base at $184,500.

Florida’s unemployment program is paid for by employers. FloridaCommerce describes the reemployment tax as “money that Florida employers pay” into the Unemployment Compensation Trust Fund, charged on the first $7,000 of each employee’s wages (updated January 12, 2026).

This calculator already includes the federal items, so the take-home column above is a fair estimate for a single filer with no extras. Subtract whatever you elect at work: health and dental premiums, 401(k) deferrals, HSA contributions, life insurance, and any court-ordered garnishment. Pre-tax elections also cut federal withholding, so enter them in the paycheck calculator. The W-4 calculator shows how each W-4 step moves the federal number.
