Pennsylvania Paycheck Calculator

In Pennsylvania, a single worker earning $65,000 a year and paid every two weeks takes home about $2,015.85 per paycheck in 2026: $216.15 federal withholding (IRS Pub 15-T), $191.25 Social Security and Medicare, and about $76.75 of Pennsylvania income tax (Tax Foundation 2026 brackets).

Does Pennsylvania have local income taxes?

Yes, and nearly every Pennsylvania worker pays one. Employers with Pennsylvania worksites must withhold the local Earned Income Tax (EIT) and the Local Services Tax (LST), according to the Department of Community and Economic Development. The Tax Foundation’s 2026 table puts the state’s average local income tax at 0.99% of AGI (2023 data). This calculator deducts neither.

The EIT rate depends on where you live and where you work. DCED provides a lookup by address for each PSD code and rate, and a Residency Certification Form you give your employer alongside your W-4.

The LST is a flat annual charge set by the municipality where you work. DCED says the total you pay in a calendar year “remains limited to $52,” however many places you work, and towns charging more than $10 must exempt people earning under $12,000 there.

Philadelphia runs its own Wage Tax instead. From July 1, 2026, the City of Philadelphia rate is 3.735% for residents and 3.425% for non-residents who work in the city. All employed residents owe it wherever they work. For a resident earning $2,500 a check, that is about $93.38.

Pennsylvania’s reciprocal agreement with New Jersey does not cover Philadelphia’s wage tax or other local taxes.

What else comes out of a paycheck in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania is one of the few states where workers pay into unemployment insurance. The Department of Labor and Industry’s 2026 tax highlights set employee UC withholding at 0.07%, or 70 cents per $1,000 of gross wages, with “no cap” on the wages it applies to. On a $2,500 check, that is $1.75. This calculator does not include it.

The state income tax itself is flat. The Department of Revenue says employers withhold 3.07% of compensation from resident and nonresident employees.

Retirement deferrals are treated differently from federal rules. The department’s gross compensation guide lists employee contributions to a qualified deferred compensation plan among items “always taxable” as Pennsylvania compensation. A 401(k) deferral lowers your federal withholding but generally not your 3.07% state tax.

Pennsylvania also has reciprocal agreements with Indiana, Maryland, Ohio, New Jersey, Virginia and West Virginia, so residents of those states working here are generally not subject to Pennsylvania withholding.

Subtract the UC withholding, your local EIT and LST, and any benefits you elect from the figures above, or use the paycheck calculator as a starting estimate.
