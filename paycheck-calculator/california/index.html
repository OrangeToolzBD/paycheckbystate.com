California Paycheck Calculator

In California, a single worker earning $65,000 a year and paid every two weeks takes home about $2,016.14 per paycheck in 2026: $216.15 federal withholding (IRS Pub 15-T), $191.25 Social Security and Medicare, and about $76.46 of California income tax (Tax Foundation 2026 brackets).

Does California have local income taxes?

No. California cities and counties do not take an income tax out of your wages, so the only income tax withheld is the state tax shown in the tables above. The Tax Foundation’s 2026 state income tax table does not list California among the states with county- or city-level income taxes.

The same Tax Foundation note (February 2026) adds one caveat: in California, as in a handful of other states, “some jurisdictions have payroll taxes.” The note does not name a California city income tax on employees. If a local line ever appears on your stub, subtract it from the estimate here, because this calculator models only the state income tax.

What does matter is the form you hand your employer. The Employment Development Department says new hires and anyone changing withholding must submit both the federal Form W-4 and California’s Form DE 4, the Employee’s Withholding Allowance Certificate. If you do not turn in a DE 4, the EDD instructs employers to withhold as if you were single with zero allowances, which can make your California withholding higher than the estimate on this page. Use the W-4 calculator to check the federal side.

What else comes out of a paycheck in California?

The biggest extra deduction is State Disability Insurance (SDI). The EDD states that “the SDI withholding rate for 2026 is 1.3 percent,” and since January 1, 2024 all wages are subject to it, with no annual cap. On a $2,500 biweekly check, that is $32.50 each payday. This calculator does not deduct SDI, so subtract it from the take-home figures above.

SDI pays for two programs. According to the EDD’s SDI overview, it provides short-term Disability Insurance and Paid Family Leave wage replacement to eligible workers who need time off. You do not see a separate family leave deduction; it is funded inside the SDI line.

Your SDI deduction grows with your pay, since there is no wage ceiling. At $100,000 a year paid every two weeks, 1.3% is about $50.00 a check.

After those, your stub shows federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare (already in the table) and anything you chose: health premiums, 401(k) deferrals, union dues. Pre-tax deferrals lower the federal figure, so run your own numbers in the paycheck calculator and treat every result here as an estimate.
