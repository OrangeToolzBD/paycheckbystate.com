New Jersey Paycheck Calculator

In New Jersey, a single worker earning $65,000 a year and paid every two weeks takes home about $2,013.95 per paycheck in 2026: $216.15 federal withholding (IRS Pub 15-T), $191.25 Social Security and Medicare, and about $78.64 of New Jersey income tax (Tax Foundation 2026 brackets).

Does New Jersey have local income taxes?

No, not on your paycheck. New Jersey municipalities do not withhold an income tax from employees, so the state tax in the table above is the only income tax besides federal. The Tax Foundation’s 2026 table leaves New Jersey off its list of states with county- or city-level income taxes, noting only that some New Jersey jurisdictions “have payroll taxes.”

Newark is the best-known example. The City of Newark says its Payroll Tax equals 1% of wages for services performed in the city, and the city’s page treats it as the employer’s tax, filed on a quarterly return.

If you commute across the Delaware River, the reciprocal agreement with Pennsylvania matters. The New Jersey Division of Taxation (page updated May 7, 2025) says pay that Pennsylvania residents earn in New Jersey is not subject to New Jersey income tax, and the reverse holds for New Jersey residents working in Pennsylvania. Pennsylvania residents file Form NJ-165 with a New Jersey employer; New Jersey residents give a Pennsylvania employer Form REV-419EX, the Employee’s Nonwithholding Application Certificate.

The agreement does not cover Philadelphia’s wage tax or other Pennsylvania local taxes, so a New Jersey resident working in Philadelphia still pays the city’s non-resident rate. See the Pennsylvania paycheck page for that rate.

What else comes out of a paycheck in New Jersey?

New Jersey workers pay into four state insurance funds, and this calculator deducts none of them. The Department of Labor’s 2026 rate information lists these worker rates:

The UI, WF/SWF, TDI and FLI lines usually appear as separate items on a New Jersey pay stub. Once your year-to-date wages pass each cap, that deduction stops. On a $65,000 salary, the UI and WF/SWF lines end partway through the year; TDI and FLI continue all year.

Your state income tax withholding is set by Form NJ-W4. The Division of Taxation says you complete it and give it to your employer, using the wage chart on the form to choose a rate. Then subtract any benefits you elect, or model them in the paycheck calculator.
