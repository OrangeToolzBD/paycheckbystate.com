North Carolina Paycheck Calculator

In North Carolina, a single worker earning $65,000 a year and paid every two weeks takes home about $2,012.41 per paycheck in 2026: $216.15 federal withholding (IRS Pub 15-T), $191.25 Social Security and Medicare, and about $80.18 of North Carolina income tax (Tax Foundation 2026 brackets).

Does North Carolina have local income taxes?

No. North Carolina cities and counties do not tax wages, so the only income taxes on a North Carolina paycheck are federal and the flat state tax. The Tax Foundation’s 2026 state income tax table (February 17, 2026) does not list North Carolina among the states with county- or city-level income taxes.

The state rate itself stepped down again this year. The North Carolina Department of Revenue’s 2026 Income Tax Withholding Tables and Instructions for Employers (Form NC-30) says that under Session Law 2023-134, “the individual income tax rate for tax year 2026 will be 3.99%.” The bracket table above uses that rate with the $12,750 single standard deduction.

Because there is no local layer, workers who move between North Carolina cities do not need to change anything with payroll for tax purposes. A move into or out of the state is different: tell your employer so that North Carolina withholding starts or stops.

If you are weighing a job offer across the state line, the state income tax rates page compares North Carolina with its neighbors, and the salary to hourly calculator puts two offers on the same basis.

What else comes out of a paycheck in North Carolina?

North Carolina withholds a little more than the 3.99% tax rate. The NC-30 tables say the 2026 percentage method is “based on the individual income tax rate of 3.99% plus 0.1%,” which “results in a withholding tax rate of 4.09%.” Supplemental pay such as a bonus can also be withheld at a flat 4.09%.

This calculator applies 3.99%, the rate on your return. Using 4.09% with the same deduction, a single filer paid every two weeks would see about $42.87 withheld at $40,000 (versus $41.82 above), $82.19 at $65,000 (versus $80.18) and $137.25 at $100,000 (versus $133.90). The extra is credited against your tax when you file your return.

Your withholding form is the NC-4, or the simpler NC-4 EZ. The NC-30 says each employee must complete one of them (or NC-4 NRA for nonresident aliens), and your employer uses it to pick the right table.

After federal tax, Social Security, Medicare and state tax, subtract any benefits you choose, such as health premiums and 401(k) deferrals, or enter them in the paycheck calculator.
