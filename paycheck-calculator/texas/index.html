Texas Paycheck Calculator

In Texas, a single worker earning $65,000 a year and paid every two weeks takes home about $2,092.60 per paycheck in 2026: $216.15 federal withholding (IRS Pub 15-T), $191.25 Social Security and Medicare, and no state income tax, because Texas does not tax wages.

Does Texas have local income taxes?

No. Texas has no state income tax on wages and no city or county income tax either. The Texas Comptroller’s A Field Guide to the Taxes of Texas (December 2025) says it plainly: Texans “pay federal income taxes but not state or local income taxes.”

That is why the Texas tax column in the take-home table above reads $0.00 at every salary. The Tax Foundation’s 2026 state income tax table (February 17, 2026) confirms the state side, listing Texas among the eight states that levy no individual income tax at all.

Because there is no state income tax, there is also no Texas withholding certificate to fill out. When you start a job in Texas, the only withholding form that sets your income tax is the federal Form W-4. If you move to Texas from a state that taxes wages, ask your employer to stop withholding for the old state once you no longer work there.

Texas raises its money in other ways, chiefly sales tax and local property tax, which do not come out of your paycheck. If you want to compare what you would keep in a state that does tax wages, the state income tax rates page lists every state’s 2026 brackets.

What else comes out of a paycheck in Texas?

In Texas, the required deductions are federal only: federal income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare. The IRS (page updated January 20, 2026) sets the employee share at 6.2% for Social Security and 1.45% for Medicare, and an extra 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax applies to wages above $200,000 in a calendar year.

Social Security stops once your wages for the year pass the wage base, which IRS Publication 15 gives as $184,500 for 2026. Medicare has no cap. On a $65,000 salary paid every two weeks, those two taxes come to $191.25 a check, as the table above shows.

This calculator already includes all three, so for most Texas workers the take-home figure above is close to the full picture. What it does not include is anything you sign up for: health, dental and vision premiums, 401(k) or 403(b) deferrals, HSA contributions, life insurance and wage garnishments. Subtract those yourself. Pre-tax items also lower your federal withholding, so enter them in the paycheck calculator for a closer estimate.

Your W-4 choices change the federal line, too. The W-4 calculator shows the arithmetic behind each step of the form.
