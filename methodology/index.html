Methodology

Every tool on this site runs in your browser from one calculation file and one data file. The data file was built on September 16, 2026 from the sources listed below. Each formula is described here in plain words, with a worked example from the site’s automated tests where one exists. The tests are checked against a separate re-implementation of the same rules.

Data sources

  • Federal withholding: IRS Publication 15-T (2026), Worksheet 1A and the Annual Percentage Method tables for automated payroll systems, both the standard and the Step 2 checkbox schedules.
  • 2026 federal brackets and standard deductions: IRS inflation adjustments for tax year 2026 (IR-2025-103, October 9, 2025): $16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly, $24,150 head of household. Head of household bracket thresholds are read from the Publication 15-T head of household table.
  • Social Security and Medicare: 6.2% Social Security up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500 (Social Security Administration); 1.45% Medicare plus 0.9% withheld on wages above $200,000 in a calendar year regardless of filing status (IRS Topic 751, updated January 20, 2026).
  • Bonus withholding: IRS Publication 15 (2026): 22% on supplemental wages, 37% on supplemental wages above $1 million in the year.
  • State income tax: Tax Foundation, State Individual Income Tax Rates and Brackets, 2026 (published February 17, 2026; law as of January 1, 2026): brackets, standard deductions, and personal exemptions or exemption credits. Washington is treated as having no tax on wages because its income tax applies only to capital gains.

Paycheck calculator

Yearly pay is your salary, or hourly rate × hours per week × 52. Gross pay per check is yearly pay divided by 52, 26, 24 or 12 pay periods. A pre-tax 401(k) is taken as a percentage of gross pay, and pre-tax health insurance as a dollar amount per check.

Federal withholding follows Publication 15-T Worksheet 1A. The tool annualizes your pay after 401(k) and health deductions and adds W-4 Step 4(a). It then subtracts Step 4(b) plus $8,600 ($12,900 for married filing jointly) unless the Step 2 box is checked. That result goes through the annual table for your filing status and Step 2 setting, and is divided by your pay periods. Your Step 3 credits, divided by pay periods, come off next (never below zero), and Step 4(c) is added.

Social Security is 6.2% of annualized pay after health premiums (401(k) deferrals stay in), capped at $184,500 and spread evenly across the year. Medicare is 1.45% of the same pay, plus 0.9% of any annualized amount above $200,000. The state estimate is described below. Take-home pay is gross pay minus the 401(k), health premium, federal withholding, Social Security, Medicare and state tax. Test: a single filer in Texas earning $65,000, paid every two weeks, takes home $2,092.60.

State income tax estimate

The tool starts from yearly wages after pre-tax deductions and subtracts the state’s standard deduction and personal exemption. It taxes the rest through the state’s brackets, then subtracts any exemption credit. Married filing jointly uses the state’s joint brackets and amounts where the state publishes them; other filing statuses use the single figures. The yearly total is divided evenly across paychecks. States with no tax on wages show zero. Test: the same $65,000 salary in California gives $76.46 per check.

Salary to hourly calculator

Paid hours per year are hours per week × paid weeks per year. The hourly rate is salary ÷ paid hours; going the other way, salary is the rate × paid hours. Monthly, biweekly and weekly figures are the yearly amount ÷ 12, 26 and 52. All figures are before tax. Tests: $25 × 40 × 52 = $52,000; $60,000 ÷ 2,080 hours = $28.85.

Income tax calculator

Federal taxable income is your income minus the 2026 standard deduction for your filing status and any pre-tax contributions or above-the-line deductions you enter. Tax is built up bracket by bracket. The marginal rate is the highest bracket reached, and the effective rate is tax ÷ income. Credits, itemized deductions and special deductions are not included. The state figure uses the estimate above on income minus the deductions you entered. Test: $80,000, single, gives $8,770 of federal tax and a 22% marginal rate.

Overtime calculator

Regular pay is rate × regular hours. The overtime rate is your rate × 1.5 or × 2, and overtime pay is that rate × overtime hours. The tool adds the two and shows the result before tax. Test: $20 an hour, 40 regular hours and 10 hours at 1.5× gives $1,100.

Raise calculator

New pay is current pay × (1 + raise %), current pay + the raise amount, or the new salary you enter. The raise percentage is the increase ÷ current pay. The extra per paycheck is the increase ÷ pay periods, before tax. Test: $60,000 plus 4% is $62,400, or $92.31 more per biweekly check.

W-4 withholding calculator

Estimated tax is the federal income tax on wages plus other taxable income, using the standard deduction for your filing status, minus the credits you enter (never below zero). Withholding per check uses the paycheck method above with default W-4 settings: Step 2 unchecked and Steps 3 and 4 blank. Projected withholding is tax already withheld + withholding per check × paychecks left. When projected withholding falls short, the gap ÷ paychecks left is shown as the extra amount per check. For two jobs or itemized deductions, the tool points to the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator.

Bonus tax calculator

Federal withholding is 22% on the part of the bonus that keeps this year’s bonuses at or below $1 million, and 37% on any part above it. Social Security is 6.2% of the bonus, limited to the room left under $184,500 after the wages you have already been paid this year. Medicare is 1.45% of the bonus, plus 0.9% of any part that takes your year’s wages above $200,000. State withholding is the bonus × the rate you enter, because the tool does not supply state bonus rates. Test: a $5,000 bonus after $60,000 of wages keeps $3,517.50.

Timesheet calculator

Each row’s hours are clock-out minus clock-in, plus 24 hours when the shift crosses midnight, minus break minutes. Rows are added for the week. Hours up to your overtime threshold (40 by default) are paid at your rate, and hours above it at 1.5×. Tests: five days of 08:30 to 17:00 with 30-minute breaks = 40 hours ($880 at $22); 22:00 to 06:00 = 8 hours.

What the tools do not model

  • Local income taxes. Tax Foundation notes that ten states have county- or city-level income taxes.
  • State payroll deductions such as disability insurance or paid family leave.
  • State tax credits other than exemption credits, and each state’s own treatment of retirement contributions. The tools subtract 401(k) deferrals from state wages the same way as federal.
  • Year-to-date wage caps as payroll applies them. The paycheck tool spreads the Social Security cap and the extra Medicare threshold evenly across the year.
  • Federal itemized deductions, and credits other than those you enter.
  • Employer rounding, benefit deductions not listed above, and wage garnishments.

Refresh schedule

The tax data is refreshed every January, once the IRS publishes the new Publication 15-T and inflation adjustments, the Social Security wage base is announced, and Tax Foundation releases its new state table. Pages show the tax year in their titles. A source that publishes a correction or a mid-year law change triggers an update outside that cycle. Every change is re-tested before it goes live.
